\begin{abstract}
The radius--period diagram of confirmed exoplanets mixes radii measured from transit depth with radii the NASA Exoplanet Archive calculates from planet mass. How does the distribution differ between the two?
We describe 6,045 catalogued planets with both radius and period, split by the archive's own label for calculated radii.
% src: estimation_results.json#sample.n_observations
Of these, 1,215 (20\%) have calculated radii and 4,810 (80\%) have radii measured on transiting planets; 20 non-transiting planets with measured radii are reported separately.
% src: estimation_results.json#groups.calculated.n, groups.measured_transiting.n, groups.measured_non_transiting.n
Calculated radii have a median of 12.6 Earth radii and 359 days, against 2.5 Earth radii and 8.0 days for transit-measured radii.
% src: estimation_results.json#statistics.radius_calculated.median, statistics.period_calculated.median, statistics.radius_measured_transiting.median, statistics.period_measured_transiting.median
The rank correlation between radius and period is 0.30 overall, 0.40 among calculated radii and 0.08 among transit-measured radii.
% src: estimation_results.json#associations.radius_period_all.estimate, associations.radius_period_calculated.estimate, associations.radius_period_measured_transiting.estimate
These are patterns in the catalogue, not in the planet population, and the study makes no causal claim.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} exoplanets; planet radius; orbital period; radius provenance; descriptive statistics
