# Methods Review: Spatial clustering of GDP per inhabitant across EU NUTS-2 regions, 2024

Scope: I reviewed the draft and the supporting documents in context (specification, data summary, plan, data dictionary). `run_estimation.py` and `estimation_results.json` were not part of the material, so I could not read the code or recompute any number. Every statement about computation below is checked against the specification and the draft only. The script review (Section A, item 1) is therefore open.

## Overall assessment

The design fits the question. It is a descriptive spatial study with global Moran's I, a Moran scatter plot, LISA with an FDR correction, Gi*, several weights definitions, and drop-one sensitivity checks. The paper keeps to association language and says that it makes no causal claim. The within-country demeaning check is the most useful part: it shows that the clustering is largely a national phenomenon. The weak points are these:

- The queen weights contain ad hoc links for 13 island regions, and the main sample's treatment of them is not tested.
- Several sensitivity checks leave isolated regions with zero-weight rows.
- The local-cluster inference needs more care.
- Several claims in the abstract and conclusion are stronger than the evidence.
- The plan and the draft do not match.

## A. Must change

1. **The script is not available to this review, and there is no recomputation.** The spatial_results check recomputes global Moran's I from the weights files. It does not verify LISA, Gi*, the Gini, the Theil decomposition, the demeaned I, or the drop-one results. The paper should state the software (numpy implementation, seed value, permutation scheme). The researcher should confirm that the script takes only local files (data.db, the CSVs) and that every `default_rng` call has a seed. The specification says "seed fixed" but does not give the value. The paper does not report it either.

2. **The demeaned Moran's I is the headline sensitivity result, and its construction is under-specified and partly fragile (Sec. 3, Sec. 4.4, abstract).**
   - The abstract says that "spatial autocorrelation disappears" once income is measured relative to the national mean. Demeaning by country mean mechanically removes between-country variance. It also induces a negative bias in Moran's I when countries have few regions. Within-country deviations sum to zero (population-weighted) within each country, and single-region countries (EE, LV, LU, CY, MT, SI and others) become exactly zero. The null expectation −1/(n−1) is therefore not the right benchmark. The permutation null is also wrong: it shuffles demeaned values across all regions, including across countries, whereas the structure that was removed is within-country. Fix: report I for the demeaned variable with a null that shuffles within countries (restricted permutation), or report I restricted to links within a country, and say how many regions have a zero value.
   - The weights matrix is the same, but about 25% of links cross a border. Those links connect deviations from different national means. A restricted version would use only within-country links, re-row-standardised.
   - The demeaning uses the population-weighted mean in the text (Sec. 3) but, according to the plan (5.6), the country mean of ln GDP. The paper and the script must agree. A level-based demeaning (Sec. 3) and a log-based one can give different results. Report both, or state which one the script uses.
   - The sentence "Within countries, high-income regions are thus not systematically adjacent to other high-income regions" is a reasonable reading of an I of 0.020 (p = 0.6). The test has low power, since many countries have few regions (more than half of the countries have fewer than 5 NUTS-2 regions). "Statistically indistinguishable from zero" is not evidence of absence. Report an interval, or the permutation distribution, and add the power caveat. The same applies to the abstract wording "spatial autocorrelation disappears".
   - A fairer decomposition would be a country-fixed-effects R² (the plan lists a one-way variance decomposition), or Moran's I of residuals from a country-fixed-effects regression. The paper uses a Theil decomposition instead, which is fine, but the paper should state that 36% between-country is a Theil share, and that the between-country share of the variance of income is a different number (the plan expected a variance decomposition of ln GDP). Show both, since the "national phenomenon" claim rests on them. Note also that a 36% between-country Theil share sits uneasily with the claim that the clustering is "almost entirely a between-country phenomenon". The two statements measure different things. The paper should reconcile them explicitly.

3. **Island links and the isolated-region problem (Sec. 2, Sec. 4.4).**
   - The 13 island links are ad hoc. The longest is Cyprus to EL42 (646 km). A link from Cyprus to Greece, or from Malta to Sicily, injects a distant value into a "contiguity" matrix. Cyprus and Malta are single-region countries, so the demeaned variable is zero there.
   - The plan (R3) promised a queen run without the island links (zero rows) and a 223-region run. The draft does not report this. The sentence "treatment of islands" in the Introduction says the check was done, but the draft shows no result for the island rule. Either report it or remove the claim. This is a must-fix because the abstract and the introduction both say the results do not depend on it.
   - The capital-drop and top-5 variants leave 3 and 1 regions with no neighbours (zero rows). With row-standardisation and a zero row, S0 falls and the statistic is computed on n including regions with a zero lag. These variants are therefore not comparable with the main I. The jump to 0.623 when the 27 capitals are dropped is the largest change in the paper, and it is described as "raises it considerably" and used to argue that capitals reduce clustering. A change of this size comes with a change in n from 236 to 209 and with a rebuilt weights matrix (dropping a capital that sits between two regions removes links and creates new islands). Fix: report n, the number of isolated regions and the mean number of neighbours for each variant. Re-link the isolated regions as in the main matrix, or drop them, so that the variants are comparable. The draft's own caveat covers this only in passing.
   - The capital-drop variant also removes whole countries (EE, LV, LU, CY, MT are single-region countries), so the sample composition changes, not only the peaks. That is a different experiment from "dropping peaks". A cleaner version drops capitals only in multi-region countries.

4. **The pseudo p-value is reported as 0.001 everywhere, and the "z" is then the only informative number.** With 999 permutations the p-value cannot go below 0.001. The plan promised 9,999 permutations and the draft and specification use 999. The plan and the draft are inconsistent, and the plan was not updated. Use 9,999 permutations (the cost is small for n = 236), or state that the reported "p = 0.001" means "p ≤ 0.001". The draft does the second in the text, but Table "moran" and the sentence "All five statistics reject the null ... at the smallest attainable p-value" suggest more precision than there is. Note also that the z for inverse distance (12.5) is larger than the z for queen (7.8) while the I is much smaller. The explanation offered (distant regions receive weight) is incomplete. A very dense matrix lowers the permutation variance of I, so z is not comparable across weights. Do not rank weights by z. Say so.

5. **Local cluster inference (Sec. 3, Sec. 4.3).**
   - Conditional permutation p-values for LISA with queen weights have a known problem. The units have different numbers of neighbours (1 to 11) and the lowest attainable p-value depends on it for small neighbourhoods. A region with one neighbour (islands linked by an ad hoc rule) has very few distinct permutation outcomes in its lag. State how many regions have fewer than 3 neighbours and whether they are excluded from the significance counts. The island links also create "neighbours" for the lag that are 600 km away.
   - Benjamini–Hochberg assumes independence or positive dependence. LISA statistics of neighbouring regions share data, so the dependence is strong. BH under positive regression dependence is defensible, but the paper should state this assumption and cite Caldas de Castro and Singer (2006), which the literature review lists but the draft does not use. Because that key is not in `literature.bib` unless registered, cite it only after running `e2er-lit search`. A sensitivity check with the more conservative Benjamini–Yekutieli correction, or Bonferroni, is a one-line addition.
   - The draft says "about 12 would be expected by chance alone" for 62 significant regions. That figure assumes 236 independent tests under the null. Under spatial autocorrelation, as in the data, a global null of no autocorrelation does not hold, and local tests are correlated, so the number of false positives is not 5% of tests. The conditional permutation null also does not describe the global pattern. Describe 12 as a rough benchmark.
   - The classification gives the 22 FDR survivors as 20 low-low, one high-high and one high-low. Name them (a table of the 22 regions with country, value, I_i, p, q). The draft names countries for the uncorrected clusters only. The abstract describes high-income clusters in Germany, the Netherlands, Ireland and northern Italy, but after correction only one high-high region survives. The abstract's sentence reads as if both sets were established. Say in the abstract that the high-income clusters do not survive correction (only one region does).
   - Gi* uses analytic z-scores with a normal reference, although the plan said permutation. The analytic Gi* z with row-standardised weights and a skewed variable is poorly calibrated. The specification admits no multiplicity correction for Gi*, and the draft says so. Either add a permutation or FDR version or drop the Gi* counts (24 hot, 30 cold) from the text, since they add no independent evidence. Note that LISA and Gi* are computed on the same weights and are not independent confirmations; "points the same way" should not be read as replication.

6. **Plan–draft mismatches that affect what a reader can trust.** The plan lists these items and the draft does not report them or says nothing about them:
   - Geary's C, the k = 3 sensitivity, the correlogram or distance profile, the trimmed and winsorised variants (R7), the other reference year (R11), and the check for unflagged-only observations. The data dictionary states that the analysis would report "as robustness, with the 96 unflagged values excluded or with 2023". The draft instead says that 134 of 236 values are provisional and 33 are estimated, but runs no check on that. Either run and report it (a simple subsample Moran's I on unflagged regions, with the weights rebuilt), or say clearly that it was not done. The data-quality statement matters because about 71% of the values are provisional or estimated.
   - The draft's "sensitivity to the treatment of islands" and "outermost regions" claims need the corresponding table rows. For outermost regions the draft reports the kNN-5 run on 244 regions (I = 0.276 against 0.266). That is a fair check, but it is for kNN only. The draft says "changes Moran's I only marginally", which is accurate for that case; do not generalise it to queen.

7. **Level versus log, and which variable carries the conclusions.** The Moran's I on levels is dominated by a right tail that includes the Irish regions and Luxembourg, whose values reflect profit shifting and commuting (the draft's own caveats). The log version raises I to 0.482 (a 30% rise). The draft treats the log as a robustness check, but the two differ enough that the headline number should be reported with both in the abstract, or the abstract should say "0.37 on levels, 0.48 on logs". A skew of this size also means that the Gini of 0.20 is on the lower side for regional data, which is population-unweighted. Say whether the Gini and the quantile ratios are region-weighted or population-weighted. The draft's text reads as unweighted for Gini, CV and quantiles but uses a population-weighted Theil. Mixing the two should be stated.

8. **The reasoning about the sensitivity results contains claims that the design does not support.**
   - "The clustering is not an artefact of a few metropolitan peaks; dropping the top five regions raises it." The top five include the two Irish regions IE05 and IE06, Luxembourg, Hamburg (DE60) and Prague. These are not "metropolitan peaks" in a uniform sense. Dropping them raises I because it removes values that are extreme and poorly aligned with their neighbours' values. That shows that the peaks lower I, not that clustering is robust to the exclusion of any influential observation. A leave-one-out influence analysis (the distribution of I when each region in turn is dropped, or Cook-type influence for the Moran regression slope) would support "not driven by a few regions" directly.
   - The explanation that "capital regions are income peaks surrounded by poorer neighbours" is labelled a reading, as the researcher asked. Keep it so. The Discussion should not return to it as a finding.
   - "The clustering is the geography of national income levels" (Discussion) is a stronger statement than the demeaned I supports, because of the points in item 2. A restricted-permutation test and the share of variance in a country-fixed-effects R² would support it. As the sentence stands, the draft rests it on one statistic with a mismatched null.
   - The paragraph on the share of queen links crossing borders (24.8%) is used to explain why the national level absorbs most of the signal. This is a plausible mechanism, but it is not a test. It also shows that the within-country test has about 75% of the links within countries, so the within-country result has real power for large countries (DE, FR, ES, IT, PL). Report the demeaned I for the five largest countries separately (descriptively); the pooled number hides heterogeneity.

## B. Should change

9. **Weights choices.** Queen contiguity, kNN 5 and 8, and inverse distance (1/d, all pairs, no cut-off) are reasonable. Inverse distance without a cut-off gives a matrix where every region has 235 neighbours, so I is close to a global gradient measure and its permutation variance is small. The plan said a distance band. State that the implementation differs from the plan and why. Add the exponent (1/d² is conventional) as a sensitivity, or drop the claim that it "remains clearly positive" as an independent confirmation. The three alternatives are all distance-based and they share the island handling problem only for queen. Add rook, or queen without the island links, so that the contiguity family has an alternative.

10. **k-NN with k = 8 and 5 gives almost identical I (0.265 and 0.266).** That is plausible, but a correlogram (I for k = 1 to 20) would show the decay of the association with distance and would make the claim about "regional gradients" testable. The plan listed it as optional. Given the paper's main interpretive claim (national blocks versus smooth gradients), it is worth adding.

11. **Cross-border structure.** The demeaned result does not distinguish national blocks from a smooth gradient that follows borders by coincidence (the researcher's correction 6 already says this). A direct check would compare I over within-country links with I over cross-border links (a join-count or a link-specific Moran decomposition), or compute the semivariogram of the demeaned values. The Discussion's statement that "it does not test for gradients across borders" is correct; the proposed NUTS-3 repeat does not address it either. A cross-border-link analysis does, and costs little.

12. **MAUP.** The paper fixes NUTS-2 and says the results would differ at other levels. It also promises, as the next question, an analysis at NUTS 3. Because the within-country result is attributed in part to the coarse size of NUTS-2 units, a NUTS-1 run (fewer, larger units; data are available in the same dataset) is a cheap check in the opposite direction. NUTS-2 units are also very heterogeneous: Germany has 38, Luxembourg 1 and Malta 1. The number of NUTS-2 regions per country is partly an administrative artefact, so the weight each country gets in the pooled I is arbitrary. State this.

13. **Measurement issues that bias the statistics, not only the interpretation.**
   - Workplace-based GDP per inhabitant creates artificial high-low and low-high pairs at commuting cores (Brussels, Hamburg, Vienna, Prague, Luxembourg). The paper lists this as a caveat in the plan; the draft's Discussion does not carry it over. The LISA outliers named in the text (BG41, EL30, BE33, DK02, DEB2) are in part products of it. Say so in Sec. 4.3.
   - PPS adjusts for national price levels only, so within-country differences in prices enter the regional values. This cuts against the demeaned analysis, which removes the national level and leaves regional price differences.
   - Irish regional GDP reflects multinational accounting. The drop-Ireland result (I = 0.342, lower than the baseline 0.370) is the one sensitivity check that lowers I, other than the demeaned version. The text says "leaves it at 0.342", which is a drop of 8%, and the sentence in the Introduction says "unchanged or stronger". Fix the wording: the Irish exclusion lowers I.

14. **Reproducibility.** The Data section states the Eurostat dataset codes, which is good. Add the data release dates (GDP release 2026-02-10, population release 2026-08-28), the sha256 of the source files (already in the data dictionary), the seed, and the script name. The population data are from a later release than the GDP data; that is fine, but say it. The GDP data release is 2026-02-10 and the provisional flags will change. A `run_estimation.py` that makes web calls would not be reproducible; confirm it reads data.db and the CSVs only. The plan also mentions "libpysal/esda/geopandas", while the interpreter has only numpy, pandas, scipy and statsmodels. Make sure that the paper does not name software that was not used.

15. **Sample and index definitions.** The population-weighted mean (40,077) is used as the "EU27 = 100" base. The draft says it uses the index version for mapping. The label must read "sample mean = 100" (the plan says so), not "EU27 = 100"; Eurostat's official EU27 value differs. Check the map legend and caption. The text "Only 86 of 236 regions exceed the population-weighted mean" is an arithmetic consequence of the skew and of the weighting, not an additional finding; "reflects the right skew" is not quite right, since the gap between the unweighted median and the population-weighted mean also reflects that large regions are richer.

16. **Wording of the Introduction.** The sentence "Our analysis yields four findings" lists "Gini ... about 0.20 ... the 90th percentile of regional income is 2.4 times the 10th". The 6.4 ratio of the maximum to the minimum is a statistic of two regions and a poor measure of dispersion; the abstract leads with it. Lead with the p90/p10 ratio. "Substantially dispersed but not extremely so" is a judgement without a benchmark; remove it or give one.

17. **Moran scatter claim.** "About three quarters of regions share the sign of their neighbours' deviation" (74 + 105 = 179 of 236 = 75.8%). The count is consistent. Note that with 31 + 26 = 57 in the off-diagonal quadrants, the proportion has no reference value: under no autocorrelation about half would be in the diagonal quadrants. Add it.

## C. Nice to have

18. Add the Geary's C line the plan promised, as a check on the global statistic's dependence on outliers.
19. Give a table of the LISA cluster membership by country and an appendix map for the FDR-corrected clusters. The plan promised these. The main text maps the uncorrected clusters (Fig. "lisa"), which is the weaker evidence. Make the corrected map the main one, or show both.
20. Show a figure of I across all variants with permutation intervals (plan Figure 6). The robustness table has the numbers; a plot helps judge the size of the differences.
21. Compare the result with Le Gallo and Ertur (2003), who found I values of similar sign for EU regions in 1980–1995. The literature review lists them but they are not citable until registered; if cited, use `e2er-lit search` first.
22. The Introduction cites a convergence-clubs paper as "does not quantify the spatial structure of regional income itself". I could not check this characterisation. Soften it unless it has been verified.

## Consistency of numbers (as far as the text allows)

- Gini 0.197 (full) against 0.194 without LU00, and 0.174 without the capitals: consistent with the claim as worded. I cannot check them against the JSON.
- 74 + 105 + 31 + 26 = 236: consistent.
- LISA counts: 19 + 38 + 2 + 3 = 62: consistent. FDR: 20 + 1 + 1 = 22: consistent.
- The country counts in the text for low-low clusters (PL 10, EL 7, BG/HU/RO 5 each = 32 of 38) leave 6 for the remaining listed countries (SK, CZ, HR, PT, southern Italy). Plausible.
- The high-high composition (5 + 5 + 3 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 19) is consistent.
- p90/p10: 53,550 / 22,750 = 2.354, reported as 2.4 and 2.35. Consistent. Max/min: 107,200 / 16,700 = 6.42. Consistent.
- The abstract and Introduction describe I "between 0.09 and 0.27" under the alternative weights: 0.089 to 0.266. Consistent.
- Mean 37,976 against the population-weighted mean 40,077: the statement that large regions are richer follows. Fine.
- Abstract: "dropping the top five regions raises it" (0.429 against 0.370). Consistent. The Introduction says dropping Luxembourg, Ireland or the top five "leaves it unchanged or stronger": Ireland gives 0.342, which is weaker. Inconsistent wording (Section B, item 13).
- The Introduction cites "Hamburg and Prague" as bright cores. The top-five list contains DE60 (Hamburg) and CZ01 (Prague). Consistent.

## Score rationale

The design is appropriate and honest about its limits, and the main global result (positive and large I that is robust to the weights definition) is credible. The main interpretive claim, that the clustering is national, rests on a demeaning test with a mismatched null and low power, and the sensitivity checks with isolated regions are not comparable with the baseline. The promised island-rule check and the provisional-data check are absent. The abstract overstates the local cluster evidence. These are fixable without new data.

OVERALL SCORE: 6/10
RECOMMENDATION: Major Revision
