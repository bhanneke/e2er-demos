Abstract

Claims that earthquakes are becoming more frequent rest on catalogue counts, which depend on detection and on the magnitude scale used. We ask how the yearly number of earthquakes of magnitude 5 and larger changed worldwide between 1976 and 2025, and whether the Gutenberg–Richter b-value is stable across decades once the completeness magnitude is taken into account. We describe more than a quarter million events of the USGS ComCat catalogue, aftershocks included, and estimate b by maximum likelihood decade by decade. The yearly count averages about 1,670 and follows no steady direction: it dips in 1996–2005 and peaks in 2006–2015. The b-value differs between decades, ranging from 0.92 to 1.22 at a common threshold, while the preferred magnitude type shifts from body-wave to moment magnitudes. Aftershock dependence and the estimated completeness magnitude make the decade comparisons descriptive. The record leaves physical change and catalogue change entangled, so decade comparisons require the threshold and magnitude type to be stated.
