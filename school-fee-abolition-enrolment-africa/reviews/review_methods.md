# Methods Review: Did abolishing primary-school fees raise gross primary enrolment? (staggered DiD, SSA)

Basis: the draft, the identification and econometric specifications, the data summary and the dictionary. I could not read `run_estimation.py` or `estimation_results.json`. Statements about the script are inferred from the specification text and the paper's source comments, and are marked as such. Scores and findings are those of a methods review. The numerical consistency check against the results file is limited to internal cross-checks.

## Summary judgement
The design is a reasonable fit for the descriptive-causal question. It uses a modern staggered estimator, reports several checks, and is candid about weak spots. The headline claim is stronger than the design supports, though. Eight treated countries, six cohorts, many with one or two countries, a comparison group of undated and possibly contaminated countries, GER breaks that coincide with treatment, and a sensitivity analysis that does not exclude zero at Mbar = 1 do not add up to the abstract's "stable" and "flat before the reform" language. Several inference and computation choices need fixing or at least explicit justification.

## Must change

1. **The pre-trend evidence is overstated (abstract, Intro, Section 5.2).**
   - The abstract says the event-study profile is "flat before the reform". The text then reports pre-period coefficients of -5.1, -5.3 and -3.6 at leads 6 to 4, with SE 5 to 8, and a Wald p of 0.98.
   - A joint p-value of 0.98 on 5 df (W = 0.76) is not evidence of flatness. It reflects huge variances from sparse and changing composition. The paper half-concedes this in Section 5.2 but not in the abstract or the intro finding 2, which says "no systematic pre-reform divergence".
   - The leads at -4 to -6 are all negative and of similar size, a pattern a rising treated-group trend would produce. Their size (about 4 to 5 points) is not small relative to the claimed 12 point impact effect for small cohorts.
   - Fix: remove "flat". State that pre-trends are uninformative at long leads, and report the composition (which cohorts and how many countries contribute at each e). Consider a balanced-composition event study (a fixed set of cohorts across e) so that movements are not compositional.

2. **Event-study base period is inconsistent with the stated method.**
   - The econometric specification says "varying base for pre-periods". The results section says "universal base". Section 4 of the paper says "universal base period g-1".
   - These give different pre-period estimates and different Wald-test and HonestDiD inputs. The statement "M = largest absolute consecutive change among pre-period estimates" is only meaningful under a defined base. With a universal base, consecutive differences are differences of levels relative to g-1 and the M is not comparable to the varying-base case.
   - Fix: state which one the script uses, and make Section 4, the econometric spec and the results consistent. Re-derive M under that choice.

3. **The sensitivity analysis is not what the abstract and intro conclude.**
   - The conservative bound scales deviation as Mbar·M·(e+1) and then adds 1.96·SE. For the overall ATT, which averages e = 0..5, the script presumably applies some average of (e+1) scaling. Which is used must be documented. A bound that grows linearly with e is a linear-trend extrapolation closer to a smoothness restriction than to relative magnitudes. It is not the Rambachan-Roth RM set. Section 5.2 calls it "conservative closed-form", but the paper then reads a "breakdown value 0.98" as if it were a property of the data. It is a property of this ad hoc bound.
   - M = 3.27 is the max absolute consecutive change among estimates that carry SE 5 to 8. It is mostly noise. Mbar = 1 means "post-treatment deviation per period as large as the largest noise-driven pre-period fluctuation". The interpretation (Section 5.2: "a deviation slightly smaller than the largest observed pre-period change is enough...") is therefore not meaningful as stated. A bound built on noisy point estimates also moves with the estimation error itself. The proper RR procedure uses the pre-period covariance.
   - Fix: implement the real RM or smoothness procedure (a conditional or hybrid CI is feasible in Python for 5 to 6 pre-periods, or port HonestDiD's LP). If not, say plainly in the abstract that the bound is ad hoc. The introduction claims the CI includes zero "once post-treatment trend deviations are allowed to equal the largest pre-treatment deviation". The abstract says "moderate violations". Align the wording. Mbar = 2 [-11.7, 55.8] should be in the text too.
   - The abstract says "The estimate is stable across ... estimators". That sits uneasily with the sensitivity finding. State it once, precisely: stable to estimator choice, not to violations of parallel trends.

4. **Inference with 8 treated clusters is not well handled.**
   - The main SE is a unit-level (pairs) bootstrap with a t(46) reference distribution. The t(G-1) df uses all 47 clusters although there are only 8 treated clusters. The effective degrees of freedom are much closer to the number of treated clusters. The paper acknowledges this ("since only 8 of the 47 clusters are treated") but then uses 46 df, which is the opposite of what the sentence implies.
   - Pairs bootstrap with 8 treated units and cohorts of size 1 gives bootstrap samples with zero treated units in some draws (probability of no treated in a draw is about (39/47)^47, tiny, but a cohort missing entirely is common). How the script handles draws where a cohort or its comparison is empty must be stated. A multiplier bootstrap is claimed in Section 4 while the specification describes "resampling units with replacement". These are different procedures. Fix the description.
   - The only small-cluster-robust result is the wild cluster bootstrap for TWFE (p = 0.062 for levels, 0.21 for logs). The headline CS estimate has no equivalent. The wild bootstrap p for TWFE is much weaker than the CS p < 0.001, which suggests that the CS inference may be too optimistic. Note that the CS SE (5.5) is far below the TWFE (9.7) and BJS (10.3) SEs for nearly the same point estimate. The paper reports this and moves on. It should explain it. Candidates: different handling of serial correlation, different weighting of the unbalanced sample, or CS bootstrap understating uncertainty by dropping the correlation between cohorts' comparison outcomes.
   - Fix: add randomization inference (listed in the pre-registration but not run) or a placebo-in-space permutation across the 47 countries (assign fake cohort structure to comparison countries). This is the natural small-treated-N check. Report the CS CI on the BJS/SA SE scale as a conservative alternative. Note that the abstract's headline significance ("1 percent level") depends on the smallest SE among five estimators.

5. **GER breaks that coincide with treatment (Section 4, Data).**
   - MWI 1995 (jump above 20 and an outlier above 150) and UGA 1997 jumps coincide with abolition. These two countries contribute the largest effects (51.8 and 41.5) and dropping UGA moves the ATT from 22.0 to 17.5. A single-year jump in a UIS series at the treatment date is what a revised enrolment or denominator break also looks like. The log robustness table cannot fix a break.
   - The "drop outliers" check drops 9 observations (text) while the data summary lists 5 flagged country-years (MWI 1995, RWA 2007-2010). This is an inconsistency to resolve (see consistency items below).
   - Fix: run the estimate dropping the jump years or using a dummy for them. Report MWI and UGA both excluded. Use enrolment counts (SE.PRM.ENRL) as the pre-registered check against denominator problems. It was pre-registered (item g) but there is no result.

6. **Comparison group contamination and the estimand.**
   - The undated comparison group contains 39 countries (the text says "39" in Section 1 and "41 economies" in Section 4; the data summary says 41 keep an empty first_year and 47 countries have GER, so 39 comparison with GER is consistent only if two are lost to missing GER; the text should say this once).
   - Many of these countries abolished or reduced fees in the same period (e.g., Ethiopia, Mozambique, Burundi, Cameroon, Benin, Madagascar, Senegal, and others the paper's own plan lists). The paper says contamination biases toward zero. That holds only if effects are homogeneous and contaminated controls have the same effect. With dynamic effects it can bias in either direction (a contaminated control that is treated later with growing effects can make the estimated effect on early cohorts appear larger or smaller depending on timing). The "not yet treated" check cannot address this, since it uses the same undated pool plus later treated cohorts.
   - Fix: at minimum, run the main estimate using only comparison countries without a documented fee policy through 2010 (hand check of the top 10 candidate countries), and report how the ATT varies. Drop the claim "bias toward zero" or qualify it.

7. **The "not yet treated" control check is nearly vacuous.** With the panel ending in 2010 and the last cohort in 2005, not-yet-treated units add very few controls (cohorts not yet treated at t among the eight). That the estimate (22.12) equals the baseline (22.04) is mechanical, since the comparison pool is dominated by the same 39 undated countries. The paper reads it as "leaves the estimate essentially unchanged". Do not count it as an independent check.

8. **Window and aggregation inconsistencies.**
   - The overall ATT averages e = 0..5. The abstract says "over the first six years", which matches e = 0..5. But the cohort composition differs at each e (GHA 2005 observed only through e = 5 at 2010, MWI through e = 16). Because the window is truncated at 5, "window [-5,5] gives identical estimate" is trivial and not a robustness check (the paper notes this in the spec but presents it as a robustness result in Section 5.3). Remove it from the robustness list or use a different window, such as e = 0..3.
   - The event-study estimate at e = 5 (27.5) rests on fewer cohorts than at e = 1. The "plateau at 22 to 27" and "persistent" language compares different cohort compositions. Provide the event study on a balanced set of cohorts or report cohort counts per e.
   - Weighted-average ATT with cohort-size weights where cohorts of Malawi and Uganda (strongest effects) are weighted by number of (g,t) cells in the window: state precisely the weights (by countries, or by observed cells).

9. **Treatment definition vs estimand.**
   - Ghana (capitation grant, not abolition), Lesotho (phased by grade) and Zambia (low confidence, one concept paper) are in the main treated set. Ghana's 2005 cohort has 5 post years and 19 pre years but the treatment is, by the researcher's own definition in the identification strategy ("capitation-grant-only reforms are not treated"), supposed to be excluded from the main spec. This contradicts the original identification strategy section 2. The revision (section 10) reintroduces them without explaining the reversal. Report the main estimate without GHA, LSO and ZMB together (only ZMB and GHA are dropped: 24.35).
   - Tanzania is coded 2002 while the source says 2001 (declared). The alternative of 2001 is promised but I see no result for it in the paper. Report it. Anticipation = 1 (21.84) is not the same thing.
   - Dates were supplied from abstracts' snippets. For Malawi, the source states "1994/95", which could be 1995, and the WDI jump is in 1995. A ±1 year re-dating check is in the paper plan but absent from results. With a 12 point impact effect at e = 0 and a MWI jump in 1995, this matters: if the true start is 1995, the e = 0 coefficient for MWI is partly a pre-period.

## Should change

10. **Gross vs net conclusion (Section 5.4, Intro finding 3).**
    - NER is observed in 684 country-years, with few pre-period observations for several treated countries. The sample is selected (post-2000-heavy). The GER-on-NER-sample is 14.8 against 22.0 on the full sample, which already shows that the NER sample is different. The paper correctly runs both on the same sample, but then writes "the gain is not driven mainly by over-age enrolment" and "a genuine increase in participation among children of primary-school age". With SE 2.5 for the difference, the 95% CI for GER minus NER is roughly [-3.6, 6.2]. NER (13.4) being 91% of GER (14.8) on this sample is suggestive. But ratios of the two levels need NER ≤ 100 compression. Also NER is bounded, so its effects at high baseline are mechanically smaller. State the CI and conclude "cannot reject equality; cannot rule out an over-age share of up to about 40%".
    - Pre-registered H2 predicted GER effect > NER effect; the data give a positive sign but insignificant. The Intro's "difference is small" is a power statement and not evidence of absence.
    - The number of treated countries contributing in the NER sample is not reported. Report it, and the cohort composition.

11. **Gender result.** The CI on the difference (0.2, SE 2.7) is about ±5.3, wide compared with plausible gender gains. "Similar for girls and boys" is fine. "Did not close the gender gap" is a different claim (gap in levels, not the difference of effects) and needs a gender parity index or the level gap. Drop or support it.

12. **Initial-level regression (Section 5.3).** Eight country ATTs regressed on pre-GER (slope -0.71, p = 0.185). Country ATTs are noisy estimates, and regressing a noisy post-minus-pre change on a noisy pre-level builds in a negative mechanical slope (regression to the mean, which the paper plan itself flagged). Also the h3 country ATT is "post minus pre", not the DiD-adjusted effect (the spec says "post minus pre"). If so, it includes the secular trend and is not comparable with the DiD ATT. It is descriptive at best; remove the sentence "consistent with larger gains where initial enrolment was lower" or add the mean-reversion caveat. Clarify which quantity is used.

13. **Covariate-adjusted specification.** The spec describes an outcome-regression CS with covariates measured at g-1. For comparison countries, the "g-1" value depends on which cohort they are matched to. State how covariates are assigned. The drop from 22.0 to 19.1 is a 13% decrease and is described as "remains significant", which is fine, but the text in the strategy section says covariates probe endogenous timing. Two controls only (GDP and urban share) is thin. Pre-treatment GER trend, or baseline GER × year, was in the pre-registration and is the most relevant control for mean reversion. Not run.

14. **Goodman-Bacon decomposition (Section 5.1).**
    - It is run on 13 countries with complete 1985 to 2010 GER, three treated cohorts (LSO, TZA, UGA) and 10 comparison. The paper uses the 95.6% weight on treated-vs-never to say "the static estimator is not distorted much". That is a statement about a sample of 13 of 47 countries, 3 of 8 treated, and not the sample used. The text says "in this subsample", good, but the conclusion for TWFE distortion in the full sample is not supported. Also note that TWFE (23.4) is similar to CS (22.0) here, so the staggered-timing worry did not bite empirically, which is a more honest reading than the decomposition.
    - The decomposition's TWFE (17.07) differs from the full-sample TWFE (23.4), by 6 points. This sensitivity to sample should be flagged.

15. **Wild bootstrap use.** The specification promises wild bootstrap-t for Sun-Abraham too; only TWFE got it. The paper does not say this. Sun-Abraham and BJS use pairs bootstrap with 499 draws. Add 999+ draws or say why 499 is enough. Bootstrap Monte Carlo error on a p-value near 0.03 (BJS) with 499 draws is non-trivial.

16. **BJS SE.** The BJS SE (10.3) is nearly double the CS SE (5.5), with similar estimates. The paper says "wider standard error" without comment. Because BJS is efficient under homogeneous trends, a larger SE suggests either a very different SE method (bootstrap on imputation with sparse early observations) or fragile imputation of the FE with unbalanced data. Investigate.

17. **Outcome transformation.** Log GER effect 0.192 corresponds to 21%, whereas the mean GER is 85.6, so 22 pp is about 26% of the mean; the paper says "about 21 percent in log terms". Fine, but note the CS in logs and levels differ in cohort weights through the base level. Also state the base for "increase of roughly 21 percent" (exp(0.1917) - 1). The statement in Section 5.1 "Because the mean GER ... is 85.6, this is a large effect" should use the pre-treatment mean of treated countries (about 78 per the data summary raw means) rather than the whole-panel descriptive mean, which includes post-reform years.

18. **Missing data and balanced pairs.** CS uses balanced pairs; with 61 gaps and four countries lacking pre-1995 data the effective sample differs across (g,t) cells. The number of comparison countries per cell, and the pre-period observations per treated cohort within the window, should be reported (the design file requires at least 3 pre-observations; I see no table). A balanced-panel re-run is pre-registered (item 6 in threats) but not reported.

19. **Multiple testing and garden of forking paths.** Pre-registered checks (randomization inference, never-treated fake dates, enrolment counts, Gardner two-stage, Sun-Abraham wild bootstrap, announcement dating, drop conflict years, window variants) are listed as run in the spec's framing but several were not run. Add a table "pre-registered vs done" so that readers see what was skipped. The paper mentions only that capitation-grant heterogeneity cannot be estimated.

## Reproducibility
- Seed 20261013 and draw counts are stated, which is good. The script and results file were not given to me; I cannot verify that the numbers come from it. Confirm that `run_estimation.py` reads dates only from `fee_abolition_dates` (not typed into the script) and that `build_panel.py` is rerunnable from data.db.
- The treatment table was built from abstracts read by the researcher, with one source lacking a DOI and one a 2020 concept paper. Provide the table as a shipped file with quotes (the dictionary mandates `source_quote_or_page`). Only DOI/reference are recorded. Add quotes.
- The data are WDI vintage 2026-10-08 (UIS). Rerunning later will produce different numbers because of revisions. State the vintage in the paper and archive the extracted CSV.
- The covariate GDP uses constant US dollars with base year set by release; record the base.
- Aid per capita was dropped from controls because of an API failure (data summary). The identification strategy still lists aid per capita as a robustness covariate. The paper does not mention that this covariate is missing. State it.
- The 1,519/1,211 and 1,006 observation counts refer to different windows (1980 to 2010 vs 1985 to 2010). The paper presents both without explaining which one enters the estimation. The estimation uses 1980 to 2010 (1,211). The mean and SD (85.6, 28.6) are from 1985 to 2010. The comparison with the estimation sample is therefore loose.

## Consistency observations (internal cross-checks only; results file not seen)
- Outliers: the data summary flags 5 country-years (MWI 1995, RWA 2007 to 2010). The paper says 9 flagged observations (and "nine year-on-year changes exceed 20 points"). The two nines may be conflated. The sample_flow comment cites `rows_ger_outlier_flag`, which may count over the full 1980 to 2010 window, while the data summary counts 1985 to 2010. Reconcile.
- Comparison group: 41 undated economies (Section 4) vs 39 comparison with GER (Section 1). Both can be true, but say so explicitly.
- The paper says "Gross enrolment rises by roughly 22 percentage points over the first six years", but e = 0..5 averages ATT(g,t), including the e = 0 value of 11.9. Fine, consistent. The "plateau 22 to 27" claim: values 23.2, 23.8, 22.0, 24.7, 27.5 are reported. "Stays between 22.0 and 24.7 through year four" is consistent.
- Zero-crossing: the Mbar = 1 interval [-0.25, 44.3] contains zero by a hair, with breakdown 0.98. Fine. It is described consistently.
- TWFE: the specification section reports p = 0.020 for the cluster-robust TWFE; the paper reports SE 9.7 and wild p = 0.062 only. Add the p = 0.020 for transparency.
- "Eight countries with sparse data": Section 1 states Ghana 2005 is a "capitation grant"; the abstract says "abolished fees". Align.
- The paper has the sentence "The coefficients ... leads four to six are negative, between -3.6 and -5.3". Spec says -5.1, -5.3, -3.6, matching. Fine.
- The Wald test is described as on "five pre-reform coefficients" (e = -6..-2). With a bootstrap covariance of 5 elements from 999 draws on cells with different composition, the covariance may be near-singular, which could explain the improbably high p = 0.98 (W = 0.76 on 5 df). State the condition number or the ridge used, if any.

## Nice to have
- Add a per-cohort ATT table with cohort size and number of pre and post observations; cohort heterogeneity is the main thing a reader of a staggered design wants to see, and the one-country cohorts show it.
- Add the synthetic DiD or a matched comparison group (initial GER, region) as a check on comparison-group relevance. Regional restriction (East and Southern Africa) was in the pre-registration.
- Show the raw event-time means of treated vs comparison countries to allow a reader to judge the jump visually, with country trajectories for UGA and MWI.
- Consider PPML or capped outcomes given GER > 100.
- Report the Monte Carlo error of bootstrap p-values.
- Provide a leave-one-cohort-out in addition to leave-one-country-out (the cohorts 2002 and 2003 have two countries each).

## Score rationale
The estimator choice, transparency about dating uncertainty and the willingness to show the sensitivity bound are strengths. The weaknesses are substantive: inference with few treated clusters is not credibly handled for the headline; the pre-trend language contradicts the evidence; the sensitivity bound is ad hoc and loosely interpreted; breaks coincide with treatment for the two cases that drive the result; the comparison group is contaminated by construction; and several pre-registered checks are missing. These are fixable with the existing data, but the present conclusions ("large", "stable", "flat") need to be restated more cautiously.

OVERALL SCORE: 5/10
RECOMMENDATION: Major Revision
