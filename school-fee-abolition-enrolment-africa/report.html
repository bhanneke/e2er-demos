Did abolishing primary-school fees in sub-Saharan African countries (staggered between the mid-1990s and the mid-2000s) raise gross primary enrolment? A difference-in-differences with World Bank enrolment data, comparing countries that abolished fees with countries that had not (yet) abolished them.

Abstract

Between 1994 and 2005, governments across sub-Saharan Africa abolished primary-school fees, yet the effect on measured enrolment is contested. We ask whether fee abolition raised the gross primary enrolment ratio (GER). We apply the Callaway--Sant'Anna difference-in-differences estimator to World Bank data on 47 countries, 1980--2010, comparing eight countries with published abolition dates (Malawi, Uganda, Lesotho, Tanzania, Zambia, Rwanda, Kenya, Ghana) with 39 countries not known to have abolished fees. Abolition raised the GER by 22 percentage points on average over the first six years, about 21 percent in logs. Pre-reform differences are indistinguishable from zero, and estimates stay between 17 and 24 points when we change the comparison group, allow anticipation, add controls, drop outliers, or omit any one treated country. A sensitivity analysis shows the estimate is not robust to pre-trend violations as large as those in the pre-period.
