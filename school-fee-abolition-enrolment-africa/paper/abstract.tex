\begin{abstract}
Between 1994 and 2005, many sub-Saharan African governments abolished primary-school fees, usually with donor support, and reported large jumps in enrolment. We ask whether these national reforms raised gross primary enrolment. We compare a small group of reforming countries with countries that have no known abolition date, using World Bank data from 1980 to 2010 and a staggered difference-in-differences design. Gross enrolment rises by about 22 percentage points on average over the first six years after abolition,

Girls and boys gain similarly. The longest pre-reform leads are negative and imprecise, and a violation of parallel trends as large as the largest pre-reform change would make the effect statistically indistinguishable from zero. The estimates capture fee removal together with the funding that accompanied it, and they indicate that these reforms widened primary access substantially.
