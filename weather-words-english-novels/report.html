Abstract

Weather is a recurring theme of the Victorian novel, yet accounts of it rest on a few canonical texts. We ask how the vocabulary of weather (rain, wind, storm, fog, snow, sun, cloud and related words) varies across English-language novels by authors born in six cohorts, 1770–1799 to 1880–1899. We count a hand-built dictionary in 84 Project Gutenberg novels and check the hits against hand-judged passages. The overall weather rate shows no detectable difference between cohorts, and the range of author rates is much wider than the range of cohort rates. Snow and ice words rise about two and a half times from the first to the fifth cohort, a suggestive pattern. Only half of the sampled fog hits describe weather, so the later rise in fog vocabulary partly reflects figurative language. A word family needs hand validation before its rise or fall is read as a change in described weather.
